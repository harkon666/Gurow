# Keep a learner's board with their Enrollment

[ADR 0027](0027-separate-task-board-organization-from-assessment.md) gives a learner's Enrollment its own Task Boards for working organization, and [ADR 0028](0028-save-task-boards-whole-against-their-own-revision.md) sets how a board is stored and saved. This decision applies the same contract to a learner's board and sets what it belongs to and how review state is shown beside it.

A learner's board belongs to one Enrollment and one Skill of that Enrollment's Version. It is stored apart from every learning record, with its own revision, and saved whole against that revision exactly like the other boards: every official Task of the Skill in that Version is on exactly one card and no other Task is, a stale save changes nothing and answers the current board, and repeating the accepted arrangement is a retry that changes nothing again. Its first opening creates Backlog, To Do, In Progress and Done and places the Skill's Tasks in the Coach's saved order: a Task with at least one valid Approval in Done, the others in Backlog. That placement only reads the Approvals, under the Enrollment lock, and records nothing. No column has a role, Done included: a save writes nothing but the board. It sends no Submission, starts no Task, and changes no Review, Approval, XP or Mastery, whatever the Skill's Access or the Enrollment's status. A locked Skill or an inactive Enrollment can still be arranged, and stays locked or inactive.

Only the Enrollment's learner reads or saves the board. The owning Coach is refused (`learner_only`), because Review works from Submissions and the learner's organization is not shared; any other Account is told the Enrollment does not exist ([ADR 0013](0013-limit-enrollment-data-visibility.md)). A published Version never changes, so a board needs no coherence with other entry points: its membership is fixed when it is created. Archived content never reaches a Version's Tasks, so it stays off the board. Identical logical Skill and Task IDs in another Version belong to another Enrollment and another board.

Unlike the other boards ([ADR 0028](0028-save-task-boards-whole-against-their-own-revision.md)), a learner's board changes that the backend has not accepted survive a reload of their tab. Their intents are kept in the tab's own session storage under the Account, Enrollment and Skill, with the revision they were based on, from the moment they are made until a save is accepted or they are discarded. Each tab owns its intents: another tab's accepted save, discard or failure never overwrites or clears them, since every tab conflicts with the others' changes anyway. A tab that is closed (after the page asks first) takes its unsaved changes with it; keeping them in shared browser storage instead would need every tab to tell which other tabs are still open before taking over their changes. After a reload they are never sent on their own, since a lost save may already have reached the backend: on the revision they were based on they are shown as not saved, for Retry or Discard; on a newer board they are a conflict, reapplied or discarded by the learner. This is possible here because a published Version's Tasks never change, so a kept intent can never wait for a Task this tab has not saved. Another Account signing in to the same browser never restores them.

Review state is shown apart from columns, as two facts the learning state derives from sent revisions only: the newest revision with its own status, and the revisions whose Approval still counts. A newer pending revision neither inherits an earlier Approval nor hides it. Reviews never move cards: a Changes Requested Task stays in Done until the learner moves it. Work is sent only by the explicit send action in a Task's board details, through the existing private-draft workflow ([ADR 0002](0002-review-immutable-submission-revisions.md)); the Skill summary lists each Task's review state read-only, so one private draft is never edited in two places at once.

Keeping the board with the Enrollment was chosen over one board per learner and Skill across Enrollments, or a board shared with the Coach, because an Enrollment is the learner's unit of work and privacy: it never mixes Versions, never shows the Coach the learner's arrangement, and never lets placement look like evidence. The cost is that a learner who joins another Version arranges its boards again.
